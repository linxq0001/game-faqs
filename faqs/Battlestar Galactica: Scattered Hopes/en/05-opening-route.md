# Battlestar Galactica: Scattered Hopes Opening Route: What To Do In Your First 3 Maps

![Battlestar Galactica: Scattered Hopes official Steam header artwork](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2535950/d29dc74a212e14249b34ebbcdf028bbec5ce7ff0/header.jpg?t=1777991759)

The hardest part of a first run is not one single system. It is the way resources, fleet status, crises, expeditions, tactical combat, and roguelite upgrades all push on each other at once. If your opening order is messy, you can run out of Tylium, enter combat with a damaged Gunstar, forget squadron ammo, and lose the fleet before the run has really started.

This route gives you a safe first-run script. Pick a forgiving starting ship, spend your first management turns in a fixed order, treat the first battle as a survival check, and use the first three maps to learn the rhythm instead of chasing perfect rewards.

## Direct Answer

For your first run, choose Callisto, start on Easy, then play the first management phase in this order: secure Tylium, repair your main Gunstar, refill squadron ammo, and check morale or fleet status. In the first battle, pause immediately, send squadrons forward to intercept Cylon Raiders, focus dangerous targets with your Gunstar, and jump as soon as FTL is ready. Do not stay for extra kills.

## 1. Opening Setup: Spend One Minute Before The Run

### Choose Callisto First

Do not start by experimenting with fragile or high-micro ships. Callisto is the safer beginner choice because it gives you a more forgiving balance of armor, squadron capacity, and combat handling. Your first goal is to understand the run loop, not to prove a risky fleet can work.

Why Callisto works for beginners:

- It gives you more room to survive positioning mistakes.
- Its squadron plan is easier to understand.
- Its repair pressure is more manageable in the first maps.

### Pick Easy Difficulty

Easy is the best learning setting for the first few runs. Scattered Hopes pressures you through several linked systems at the same time: resources, morale, ship maintenance, crisis events, real-time combat with pause, and permanent upgrades. If you start too high, you may lose before you understand which decision actually caused the collapse.

On Easy, focus on three habits:

- Read the fleet status before spending a turn.
- Pause at the start of every battle.
- Jump when survival matters more than damage.

### Accept That Early Losses Are Part Of Progress

This is a roguelite. Losing early runs is not wasted time if you learn why the fleet failed and unlock stronger foundations for the next attempt. Avoid restarting just because the opening is not perfect. If Tylium, the Gunstar, and morale are still recoverable, keep going and use the run as practice.

## 2. First Management Phase: Follow This 4-Turn Order

The first management phase is where many new players overreach. You do not need to trigger every event on the map. You need to enter the first battle with fuel, armor, squadrons, and morale under control.

### Turn 1: Send An Expedition To A Green Tylium Point

Tylium is your FTL lifeline. If you cannot jump, the Cylon pursuit catches you. Your first action should usually be a high-probability Tylium expedition, especially if the map offers a green resource point.

Turn 1 rules:

- Prioritize reliable Tylium over unknown events.
- Do not spend the first turn chasing risky rewards.
- Once the next jump is safe, you can look for supplies or salvage.

### Turn 2: Repair Your Main Gunstar

After securing basic fuel, repair your main Gunstar before spending salvage elsewhere. Civilian ships matter, but a damaged Gunstar can end the run in the next fight. Early salvage should protect the ship that keeps the fleet alive.

Repair priority:

- If the Gunstar armor is not safe, repair it first.
- Only patch civilian ships if they are in immediate danger.
- Do not spend early salvage on optional upgrades before the first battle.

### Turn 3: Refill Squadron Ammo And Check Squadron Readiness

Squadrons are your first defensive layer. They intercept Cylon Raiders, protect civilian ships, and buy time for your Gunstar to handle larger threats. If you enter combat with weak or under-supplied squadrons, you will be forced to tank damage you could have prevented.

Check before combat:

- Are your squadrons filled or close to filled?
- Is ammo ready?
- Are your Vipers or other balanced units available?
- Is any key squadron too damaged to rely on?

### Turn 4: Check Morale And Fleet Status

If morale is still stable, you usually do not need to spend heavily here. Use the last turn to confirm there is no immediate crisis waiting to explode. Your goal is to enter the first fight without a hidden failure already unfolding.

If something is wrong, use this priority order:

1. Repair a badly damaged Gunstar.
2. Fix a Tylium shortage.
3. Stabilize morale if it is near a danger point.
4. Ignore low-threat crew friction until the fleet is safer.

## 3. First Battle: Survival Beats Full Clears

Do not treat the first battle as a full wipe objective. Your win condition is to keep the fleet alive until FTL is ready, then leave. A clean escape is better than an expensive victory.

### Pause Immediately

Pause as soon as combat begins. Look at the Raider approach, then send squadrons forward to intercept. Your first priority is keeping enemy fighters away from civilian ships.

Opening battle actions:

- Pause.
- Move squadrons forward.
- Keep the Gunstar near the center of the formation.
- Keep civilian ships behind or beside the protected line.

### Focus High-Threat Targets With The Gunstar

Do not waste Gunstar fire chasing every low-health enemy. Use the main ship to pressure the most dangerous Cylon target while squadrons manage Raiders. If a weakened enemy is moving away from your formation, let it go.

Combat reminders:

- Do not chase enemies so far that your formation breaks.
- Pause again if civilian ships are threatened.
- Keep squadrons close enough to return to defense.

### Jump The Moment FTL Is Ready

When FTL reaches 100%, jump. Do not stay for one more kill, one more drop, or one more clean-up target. New players lose many runs by turning a successful escape into a drawn-out fight.

For the first battle, survival is the reward.

## 4. Maps 2 And 3: Repeat The Safe Loop

After the first fight, keep the rhythm boring on purpose. The second and third maps are still about building consistency, not showing off.

### Keep Prioritizing Fuel, Supplies, And Repairs

At the start of each management phase, check Tylium and supplies first. Tylium keeps the fleet moving, while supplies give you room to handle morale and smaller crises. Then repair the Gunstar, refill squadrons, and only then take lower-priority events.

Safe management order:

1. Search for Tylium.
2. Search for supplies.
3. Repair the Gunstar.
4. Refill squadrons and ammo.
5. Handle low-risk crises or small resource points.

### Use The Same Combat Plan

For the next two battles, keep using the same approach: pause, intercept, focus major threats, and jump when FTL is ready. Once you can clear the first three maps consistently, you can start experimenting with more aggressive combat routes.

### Aim For Your First Permanent Upgrade

The real value of the first three maps is not a perfect run. It is reaching enough progress to unlock permanent upgrades and make the next run easier. Prioritize upgrades that improve survival before damage spikes.

Strong early upgrade goals:

- More Gunstar armor.
- Better fuel efficiency.
- Higher supply capacity.

These upgrades are not flashy, but they reduce the chance that your next opening collapses before you can react.

## Opening Checklist

Before entering the first battle, confirm:

- You have enough Tylium for the next jump.
- Your main Gunstar armor is safe.
- Squadrons are filled or close to filled.
- Ammo is not dangerously low.
- Morale is not near collapse.
- No high-risk crisis needs immediate attention.

If most of this checklist is clean, go to battle. Do not wait for a perfect setup. The goal is to move through the early loop with enough stability to keep learning.

## Quick Memory Line

Fuel secured, armor repaired, squadrons ready, ammo stocked. Defend first, jump fast, do not chase kills, build toward upgrades.
