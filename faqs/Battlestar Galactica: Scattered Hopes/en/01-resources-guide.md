# Battlestar Galactica: Scattered Hopes Resources Guide: Fuel, Supplies, Salvage, Ammo, And Emergency Materials

![Battlestar Galactica: Scattered Hopes official Steam page background artwork](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2535950/4a43a83c8bf5aeee2084591c79559a664f93dc6c/page_bg_raw.jpg?t=1777991759)

In Scattered Hopes, most early failures are not caused by one bad fight. They happen because resources were spent in the wrong order. If Tylium runs dry, FTL stops. If supplies collapse, morale follows. If salvage disappears into the wrong hull, your main Gunstar enters battle half-broken.

## Direct Answer

Treat Tylium as your first survival resource, supplies as your morale buffer, salvage as your fleet durability budget, ammo as combat uptime, and special materials as emergency tools. In the first maps, keep fuel above panic levels, repair the main Gunstar before side assets, and do not burn rare materials on low-impact events.

## 1. The Five Core Resources

### Tylium Comes First

Tylium is your FTL lifeline. If you cannot jump, the Cylon pursuit catches you sooner or later. That makes fuel the first resource you should stabilize on most maps.

- Prioritize green resource points early.
- Scrapping for fuel is usually better than gambling on low-value events.
- Avoid trading away your future jump path unless the run is already desperate.

### Supplies Keep Morale And Recovery Alive

Supplies are what let the fleet breathe between crises. They support morale recovery, emergency treatment, small repairs, and the general ability to absorb mistakes without an immediate spiral.

- Keep a buffer instead of spending to zero.
- Refill before morale becomes a crisis.
- Weigh refugee or event choices against future supply pressure.

### Salvage Protects Real Combat Power

Salvage is not just upgrade currency. Early on, it is your armor insurance. If your main Gunstar is damaged and salvage is low, the next battle becomes much harder than it needs to be.

- Repair the main Gunstar first.
- Delay low-value hull spending when combat pressure is rising.
- Use extra salvage on upgrades only after survivability is stable.

### Ammo Supports Battle Tempo

Ammo is less glamorous than upgrades, but a fleet that cannot keep firing loses control quickly. Squadron uptime matters especially in the early game, because they are your first line against Raider pressure.

- Refill before battle instead of stockpiling blindly.
- Prioritize squadron readiness over excessive gun waste.
- Do not enter a fight assuming you can improvise around empty launch capacity.

### Special Materials Are Emergency Resources

Tech and medical kits are the kinds of resources that feel optional until the wrong event appears. They are best treated as answers to dangerous situations, not as convenience spend.

- Save them for infiltration, crisis, or key crew emergencies.
- Do not use them to smooth over small problems.
- Think of them as run-saving leverage, not bonus loot.

## 2. A Safe Spending Order

Your spending order matters as much as your total inventory. A modest stockpile used well is stronger than a rich run managed badly.

### Fuel First, Then Stability

Before making any ambitious choice, check whether you still have enough Tylium to keep the route alive. After that, spend on the pieces that prevent a collapse in the next one or two turns.

### Repair The Main Gunstar Before Side Assets

New players often overspend trying to make every ship look healthy. That is a trap. The main Gunstar protects the fleet. If it is weak, the rest of the fleet becomes harder to protect anyway.

### Let Supplies Protect Morale

Do not wait for morale to fully crash before using supplies. A moderate resource spend at the right time is often cheaper than clawing back from a full morale emergency.

## 3. Beginner Rules That Prevent Resource Collapse

- Keep a Tylium reserve instead of treating every jump as your last safe spend.
- Use salvage for the ship that wins or loses the next battle.
- Refill ammo before a fight rather than after the damage is already done.
- Hold special materials for emergencies, not routine cleanup.
